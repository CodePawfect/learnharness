## Confidence — a self-report, interpreted with reasoning

Multiple-choice answers may carry `confidence`: the learner's stated probability, as a percentage, that the selected answer is correct. The slider defaults to 50, so that value may be untouched. Free-recall answers and "I don't know" need not carry confidence.

Use confidence as a clue alongside the actual answer, reasoning, and prior evidence:

- Correct with high confidence: encouraging immediate evidence; advance when the reasoning supports it, without claiming mastery.
- Correct with low confidence: a possible guess or uncertainty; use a different application if resolving that uncertainty matters.
- Wrong with high confidence: investigate a possible misconception or a faulty question/key before deciding what to repair.
- Wrong with low confidence: distinguish a gap from a slip using the learner's words; do not assume a small correction always suffices.
- "I don't know" (`dontKnow: true`): an explicit request for help or signal of uncertainty, without a chosen answer to grade.

Do not infer a specific belief from a confidence threshold alone. Question format, multiple correct options, default slider values, and ambiguous wording can all affect the number. Confidence never upgrades the review system's demonstrated/retained state.
