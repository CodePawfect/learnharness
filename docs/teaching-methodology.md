# Teaching and learning in the CodePawfect fork

The current objective is understanding that the learner can use independently and retrieve later. Immediate clarity is useful feedback, not evidence of permanent retention or correctness.

1. Agree on a measurable capability and available time. Read previous dated evidence; a covered concept is not proof of mastery.
2. Diagnose relevant prerequisites. State definitions, assumptions, empirical evidence, and limitations accurately. Do not force conditional claims into universal truths.
3. Adapt the method: worked examples with fading support, guided questions, concise explanation, or a project with observable acceptance criteria. The learner may request another method, more explanation, or a break.
4. Check immediate comprehension with informative application or explanation tasks. Record tutor judgments only with actual learner quotes; keep them distinct from independent checks.
5. Propose recall and fresh application problems, committing an answer or rubric and a real source before use. The learner checks the key against that source in Review.
6. Revisit at meaningful delays. Review history records answers, assistance, grading method, timestamps, and immutable answer keys. Do not equate a self-rating or model judgment with verified mastery.
7. Evaluate delayed performance, including unsuccessful and assisted attempts. Report limitations and avoid causal conclusions from a personal observational history.

The original upstream theory emphasized self-evident foundations and a single teaching cadence. This fork retains connected explanations, prerequisite planning, active thinking, and adaptive practice while removing claims that understanding cannot be forgotten or that one method is universally best.

See [implementation and state semantics](evidence-based-learning.md) and [evaluation protocol](learning-evaluation.md). The scheduler's intervals and evidence thresholds are explicit product heuristics and require empirical calibration.
